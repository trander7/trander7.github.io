Project 06 | Material Handling & Surface Protection

5 ft x 10 ft Sheet Flipper Modification

Modification of an existing sheet-handling fixture to accommodate larger stainless-steel sheets while improving control during material handling and laser-loading operations.

Project Overview

Modified an existing sheet-flipping fixture that was originally designed for 5 ft x 8 ft sheets so it could accommodate 5 ft x 10 ft stainless-steel sheet stock.

The fixture provides a controlled method for flipping large sheets before laser loading, allowing the shop to control which surface of the stainless sheet is exposed during subsequent handling.

The modification extended the capability of an existing piece of material-handling equipment rather than requiring a completely new fixture.

Impact

Expanded sheet-handling capability while supporting surface-quality control before laser cutting.

Problem Addressed

  • Existing fixture was configured for 5 ft x 8 ft sheet stock
  • Production needed to handle larger 5 ft x 10 ft stainless sheets
  • Large sheets required controlled support during flipping
  • Sheet-surface condition needed to be considered during handling

Design Response

  • Modified the existing fixture for 5 ft x 10 ft sheet compatibility
  • Extended the frame and sheet-support arrangement
  • Retained the basic operating concept of the original equipment
  • Maintained a controlled method for flipping and positioning sheets

Production Benefits

Larger Sheet Capability

Extended the fixture from its original 5 ft x 8 ft application to accommodate 5 ft x 10 ft sheet stock.

Surface Control

Provides greater control over which sheet surface is exposed during handling and preparation for laser loading.

Controlled Handling

Provides a dedicated method for flipping large stainless-steel sheets rather than relying on less controlled handling methods.

Existing Equipment Improved

Expanded the capability of an existing fixture instead of replacing the established equipment concept.

Manufacturing Focus

The project focused on adapting an existing shop-floor tool to a new production requirement. The modification allowed larger sheet stock to be handled while preserving the functionality of the original fixture concept.

Mechanical Design

The CAD assembly was used to define the extended frame, support members, pivoting structure, bracing, and overall arrangement needed to accommodate the larger sheet format.
