Project 06 | Material Handling & Process Improvement

5 ft × 10 ft Sheet Flipper

Modification of an existing material-handling fixture to accommodate larger stainless-steel sheet stock while maintaining controlled handling of the designated finished surface.

Project Overview

Modified an existing sheet-flipping fixture originally configured for 5 ft × 8 ft sheet stock so it could accommodate 5 ft × 10 ft stainless-steel sheets.

The fixture provides a controlled method for reorienting large sheets before laser loading while limiting unnecessary handling of the designated finished surface.

Rather than replacing the existing equipment with an entirely new concept, the proven fixture arrangement was adapted for the larger sheet format.

Impact

Expanded the shop's sheet-handling capability while supporting controlled handling of larger stainless-steel sheets prior to laser cutting.

Problem Addressed

  • Existing fixture was configured for smaller 5 ft × 8 ft sheets
  • Production required handling of 5 ft × 10 ft stainless-steel sheets
  • Large sheets required controlled support during reorientation
  • Finished surfaces needed to be protected during handling

Design Response

  • Extended the existing fixture concept for 5 ft × 10 ft material
  • Maintained the established controlled flipping method
  • Preserved support of the sheet during reorientation
  • Adapted existing equipment rather than developing a completely new handling system

Handling Concept

The fixture provides a controlled sequence for managing large sheet stock while preparing the material for downstream processing.

01 Load Large stainless-steel sheet stock is positioned within the support fixture.
02 Reorient The fixture controls the sheet while the material is flipped to the required orientation.
03 Transfer The correctly oriented sheet can then be prepared for loading into the laser-cutting process.

Engineering Considerations

  • Overall 5 ft × 10 ft sheet dimensions
  • Support across the larger sheet area
  • Fixture stability during reorientation
  • Material clearance through the flipping motion
  • Protection of finished stainless-steel surfaces

Production Value

  • Accommodated larger sheet stock
  • Reused an existing proven fixture concept
  • Improved control during sheet handling
  • Supported surface-quality requirements
  • Avoided unnecessary replacement of existing equipment
