Project 05 | Equipment Protection & Impact Mitigation

Shock-Absorbing Trolley End Stop

Development of an energy-absorbing end-stop concept for an overhead trolley system to reduce abrupt impact loading and protect trolley components.

Project Overview

Developed a shock-absorbing end-stop concept for overhead trolleys traveling on an I-beam track.

If a manually positioned track switch is not changed, a loaded trolley can contact the existing rigid end stop and create an abrupt impact.

The proposed design incorporates an industrial shock absorber into the end-stop assembly to introduce a controlled deceleration stroke before the trolley reaches the positive mechanical stop.

Impact

Increased stopping distance, reduced peak impact loading, and lower risk of damage to trolley components.

Problem Addressed

  • Loaded trolley can reach the end of the track when the track switch is not repositioned
  • Existing rigid stopping condition produces an abrupt impact
  • Impact loads are transferred directly into the trolley and supporting structure
  • Repeated impacts create the potential for equipment damage

Design Response

  • Industrial shock absorber aligned with trolley travel
  • Structural mounting arrangement for the shock absorber
  • Dedicated contact surface for controlled engagement
  • Additional travel used to decelerate the trolley
  • Positive mechanical stop retained behind the absorber

Engineering Basis

The design uses additional stopping distance to dissipate trolley kinetic energy over a controlled stroke rather than stopping the trolley abruptly at a rigid interface.

Shock-absorber selection and design validation depend on the moving mass, impact velocity, available stroke, allowable force, impact frequency, and rated energy capacity of the selected absorber.

Mechanical Integration

The concept was developed around the existing trolley and track arrangement. CAD was used to evaluate the available space, contact alignment, mounting geometry, and relationship between the proposed shock absorber and the existing end-stop structure.

Fail-Safe Stop

The concept retains a positive mechanical stop behind the energy-absorbing element. The shock absorber provides the controlled deceleration function while the mechanical stop remains part of the end-stop arrangement.

Portfolio Significance

  • Identified a recurring equipment-damage mechanism
  • Developed a passive equipment-protection concept
  • Evaluated the existing configuration before developing the solution
  • Integrated new hardware with an existing trolley and track system
  • Used CAD to review interfaces, alignment, and mounting geometry
  • Applied energy-management principles to a practical shop-floor issue
