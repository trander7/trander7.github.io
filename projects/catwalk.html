Project 07 | Structural Integration & Maintenance Access

Overhead Trolley Maintenance Catwalk

Elevated access structure developed around an overhead trolley network to improve access for inspection, service, and equipment maintenance.

Project Overview

Designed a mezzanine-style catwalk from the ground up to provide elevated access to overhead trolleys serving the coating and oven areas.

The original purpose included providing closer access to the trolley system for applying protective foil as well as performing maintenance. After the process was changed so grounding could be completed inside the booths, the foiling requirement was eliminated.

The completed structure remained useful as a dedicated maintenance platform for inspection, service, and repair of the overhead trolley equipment.

Impact

Improved access to the overhead trolley system for inspection, component replacement, and recurring maintenance activities.

Design Response

  • Fixed elevated platform positioned beneath and alongside the overhead trolley system
  • Integrated stair access to the elevated work area
  • Perimeter guardrails around exposed platform edges
  • Structural framing and bracing supporting the elevated platform
  • Working area positioned close to the trolley path for inspection and service
  • Permanent access solution that remained useful after the original process requirement changed

Current Maintenance Uses

  • Routine trolley inspection
  • Bearing replacement
  • Wheel replacement
  • Repair of damaged trolley components
  • Preventive maintenance
  • Corrective maintenance along the trolley system

Engineering Approach

The platform was designed around the existing trolley system and production-area constraints. The structure combines elevated working access, stairs, guardrails, support framing, and bracing into a permanent maintenance structure.

Adaptability

Although the original need for trolley foiling was later removed, the platform continued to provide useful access for regular trolley inspection and maintenance. The structure therefore remained useful even after the process that originally drove the project changed.
